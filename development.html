Development

Progress is measured by resolved risk.

A credible programme does not advance because a prototype looks complete. It advances when predefined technical, usability, evidence and quality questions have been answered.

Stage gates

Each phase must earn the next one.

Dates matter, but they do not replace exit criteria. A delayed answer is preferable to scaling an unresolved design assumption.

01

Define

Intended use and product boundary

Outputs: target users, use environment, claim ledger, preliminary hazards and measurable requirements. Exit: the proposition can be stated without relying on unverified mechanism language.

02

Build

Integrated prototype and configuration control

Outputs: traceable architecture, interfaces, software state model and controlled prototypes. Exit: the system can reproduce intended functions under defined conditions.

03

Verify

Performance, safety and usability

Outputs: bench tests, fault tests, electrical and environmental evidence, formative usability work and closed risk controls. Exit: specifications and safety-related tasks meet acceptance criteria.

04

Validate

Claim-specific evidence

Outputs: protocol, population, comparator, endpoints, analysis plan and controlled product version. Exit: evidence supports the exact claim in the intended use context.

05

Scale

Quality, authorisation and support

Outputs: manufacturing controls, supplier controls, complaint handling, service, market access and adoption economics. Exit: the organisation—not only the prototype—is ready.

Quality architecture

Quality begins before commercial manufacture.

Design history, risk management and configuration control are development tools, not paperwork added at the end.

For any medical-device pathway, the programme should align early with the applicable quality system, risk-management and usability-engineering expectations. In the United States, the FDA Quality Management System Regulation became effective on 2 February 2026 and incorporates ISO 13485:2016 by reference.

Q

Traceability

Link user needs, design inputs, risks, outputs, tests and changes.

R

Risk control

Identify hazards, control risk, verify controls and monitor residual risk through the lifecycle.

U

Use safety

Study intended users, environments, critical tasks, foreseeable misuse and recovery from error.

Regulatory divergence

The claim controls the pathway.

A “wellness” label does not automatically remove a product from device regulation. Authorities examine intended purpose, claims, instructions, design, operation and risk. The pathway must therefore be assessed separately for each jurisdiction and product version.

Stop condition

Do not begin validation, tooling or market launch while the intended use, controlled configuration or highest-risk use scenarios remain unstable.
