Evidence ladder

Each level creates a commercial asset.

The strategy avoids one all-or-nothing clinical trial. It sequences verification and claims so the product can learn without jumping ahead of the evidence.

Level 1

Engineering claims

Controlled stimulation, consistent placement, guided sessions, recorded completion and fit-adapted interface—available only after verification.

Level 2

Immediate experience

Facial activation, subjective ease, routine satisfaction, readiness to re-engage and awake-looking appearance.

Level 3

Cosmetic claims

Temporary appearance of lift, vitality, visible tone or contour, and measurable target-muscle engagement.

Level 4

Affective claims

Momentary positive valence, interpretation of ambiguous or neutral expressions and short-term physiological response.

Level 5

Future regulated applications

Clinically meaningful stress, mood, neuropsychiatric or rehabilitation outcomes—only after appropriate evidence and regulatory strategy.

18-month value-creation plan

Five stages. Explicit gates.

Months 0–3

Define

  • Intended purpose and claim architecture
  • Regulatory consultations
  • Product requirements and fit strategy
  • Preliminary economics
Months 4–6

Verify

  • Bench testing and contact reliability
  • Target engagement
  • Comfort and fitting repeatability
  • App onboarding and early human factors
Months 7–9

Build demand

  • Studio recruitment and closed demonstrations
  • Waitlist and price testing
  • Creator advisory group
  • Founders Programme preparation
Months 10–12

Prove

  • 300–500 paid early users
  • Product-specific endpoints
  • Repeat-use and complaint data
  • Manufacturing feedback and claim selection
Months 13–18

Launch

  • Controlled direct-to-consumer launch
  • Premium partners
  • Validated first claim
  • Next-market regulatory preparation

Research engine

Target engagement

Facial-muscle activation, facial action coding, EMG or related objective measures, and stimulation-versus-comfort optimisation.

Immediate affective response

Valence, intensity, perception of neutral and ambiguous expressions, heart rate and electrodermal measures.

Beauty and presence

Awake-looking appearance, perceived vitality, temporary lift, symmetry, camera and presentation readiness.

Human factors

Fit reliability, independent onboarding, tolerability, use error, adherence and repeat-use behaviour.

Research improves claims→Claims improve conversion→Users generate data→Data improves protocols

Failure conditions

The hardest requirement is not stimulation. It is repeatable target engagement with a benefit users notice.

The programme should stop, narrow or redesign if one of the following gates fails.

01

Fit fails

Too many users cannot achieve reliable contact without assisted fitting.

02

Comfort constrains dose

Tolerable settings do not produce consistent intended activation.

03

Benefit is invisible

Users or blinded assessors cannot detect a meaningful immediate outcome.

04

Repeat use collapses

Interest does not convert into sustained week-eight behaviour.

05

Economics break

Fit complexity, returns, support or consumables make the model unattractive.

06

Claims trigger a different pathway

Marketing language creates regulatory obligations the launch plan cannot support.

Early indicators

Signals to watch before consensus arrives.

The most informative indicators are operational, not promotional.

Setup success

Independent users achieve confirmed contact and fit on the first or second attempt.

Target-engagement rate

The intended muscle activation is objectively confirmed within tolerable settings.

Week-eight retention

Paid users continue using the routine without intensive vendor support.

Claim conversion

A product-specific validated benefit materially improves willingness to pay and conversion.

Regulatory clarity

Intended purpose and promotional language remain coherent across Singapore, Canada, the EU and the United States.
