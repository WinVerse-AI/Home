The WinVerse™ standard

Design ambition is useful. Uncontrolled claims are not.

01

Separate mechanism from marketing

Facial functional electrical stimulation, vagus-nerve stimulation and general-wellness positioning are different propositions. Each needs its own evidence and regulatory analysis.

02

Treat specifications as testable targets

Current, pulse width, runtime, materials, safety logic and dimensions remain engineering assumptions until prototypes and verification records support them.

03

Build the commercial boundary early

The first product narrative must be consistent with intended use, user risk, available evidence and the jurisdiction in which the product is offered.

Flagship development programme

SerotoniX™

A modular wearable concept combining a personalised facial interface, multi-channel stimulation electronics, sensing, control and a compact external controller. The architecture is under development; the boards below do not establish clinical benefit, safety certification or market authorisation.

Product thesis

A personalised interface can only matter if contact quality, comfort, repeatability and electrical safety are engineered as one system.

The quiet bottleneck is not the shape of the mask. It is reliable delivery across different faces, sessions and real-world use conditions.

User layer

Fit, comfort and routine

Scan-derived geometry, strap design, ventilation and a guided interface must reduce set-up burden rather than merely look personalised.

Engineering layer

Controlled stimulation

Channel independence, contact monitoring, charge balance, fault handling and traceability must be verified before performance language is used.

Evidence layer

Claim-specific validation

Any future wellbeing or medical proposition needs evidence generated on the actual configuration, target population and intended use.

System architecture

Four layers. One verification chain.

A credible product is not the sum of attractive components. It is a controlled chain from electrode contact to stimulation output, sensing, software, user interaction and failure response.

  1. 01
    Personalised facial interface

    Geometry, materials, ventilation, contact placement and repeatable donning.

  2. 02
    Electrode cassette

    Conductive paths, channel mapping, serviceability and interface integrity.

  3. 03
    Controller and power

    Output control, isolation, battery management, sensing, data logging and communications.

  4. 04
    Evidence and configuration control

    Every claim tied to a versioned device, protocol, test method and jurisdiction.

Evidence boundary

What the public record supports—and what it does not.

UHN/KITE has publicly described research using facial functional electrical stimulation to activate muscles associated with a Duchenne smile. A registered proof-of-concept study and a 2025 protocol concern take-home FES for major depressive disorder. That record does not validate the SerotoniX™ mask shown here, establish a vagus-nerve mechanism, confirm the displayed specifications, or prove a consumer benefit.

Supported

Research exists on facial-muscle FES

Public UHN/KITE material and the study protocol describe electrical stimulation of facial muscles as the mechanism under investigation.

Incomplete

Clinical benefit remains under study

A protocol and trial registration establish a planned investigation, not a completed efficacy finding for this product.

Unverified

Vagus-nerve stimulation claim

The supplied boards use VNS language, but the cited public programme concerns facial-muscle FES. These mechanisms cannot be treated as interchangeable.

Unverified

Performance, safety and standards

Current ranges, battery life, material grade, IEC/ISO references, closed-loop claims and dimensions are design targets until documented testing supports them.

Development sequence

The order matters.

Do not use a polished concept board to skip unresolved engineering and evidence work.

  1. 01
    Lock the intended mechanism

    Define whether the system is facial-muscle FES, peripheral-nerve stimulation or another modality.

  2. 02
    Build a controlled prototype

    Freeze hardware, firmware, electrodes, materials and configuration identifiers.

  3. 03
    Verify safety and output

    Test electrical limits, contact detection, fault handling, thermal behaviour, battery and EMC assumptions.

  4. 04
    Validate fit and use

    Measure donning, comfort, repeatability, cleaning, misuse and performance across representative users.

  5. 05
    Generate claim-specific evidence

    Study the final configuration in the intended population with endpoints that match the proposed claim.

  6. 06
    Enter each market deliberately

    Align intended use, labelling, advertising, quality system and regulatory pathway by jurisdiction.
